The glute bridge

It switches the hip's engine back on.

Filming soonThe instructions are all here in the meantime.

Film on its way

Set up

  • Lie on your back, knees bent
  • Feet planted flat on the floor further out from you than feels natural, almost awkwardly far away, rather than tucked in close to your backside
  • Feet about hip-width apart
  • Arms resting by your sides

The movement

Squeeze your glutes and press down through your heels to lift your hips off the floor, until your body makes one straight line from your knees to your shoulders. Squeeze hard at the top, hold for a moment, then lower slowly back down. The lift comes from the glutes driving through the heels, not from cranking your lower back into an arch.

What to get right

  • The lift coming from the back of you, the glutes leading and the hamstrings chipping in behind them
  • Felt in your backside and the back of your thighs, not in the front of your thighs and not as an ache in your lower back
  • Heels planted, toes light
  • A proper held squeeze at the top rather than a quick bounce

How much

  • 2 to 3 sets of 10 to 15, controlled, with a real squeeze and a pause at the top of each

Harder

  • One leg at a time, once both together feel easy. A big jump in load, and it evens up your two sides into the bargain
  • Free ankle crossed over the working knee, figure-four style, is the easier of the two
  • Free leg held straight out and aloft is the harder, because the working leg gets no help at all

Why the hip drives so much

The most powerful joint you own, and the most easily missed. It hurts on its own account, and it drives trouble into the knee and the back without a sound.
