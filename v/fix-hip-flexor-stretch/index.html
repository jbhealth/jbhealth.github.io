Hips

From Make a Fuss · Chapter 10
The most powerful joint you own, and the most easily missed. Close to how I work with clients: the same fixes, picked for what their own reading turned up and rebuilt as it changes.
⌄
1Free the front
Standing hip-flexor stretch
About 30 seconds, 2 to 3 times each sideA wall
Set up
  • Stand tall in a long split stance
  • One foot a good stride forward and flat on the floor
  • The other foot back with its heel lifted
  • Weight shared through both
The movement

Before you move forward at all, tuck your tailbone under so the front of your pelvis rolls up, and squeeze the glute of the back leg. Now, holding that tuck and that squeeze, ease your hips forward, letting the front knee bend a little, until the stretch builds across the front of the back hip. Hold there, tall and steady, then ease back.

What to get right
  • The glute squeeze and the tucked pelvis. They're the whole point
  • The upper body upright throughout, no leaning back and arching
  • The pull at the front of the hip and the top of the thigh, nowhere else. Feel it in your lower back and you've lost the tuck and the squeeze, so reset and try again

Easier

  • If a tender knee can't take the standing version, take it to the floor. Kneel on the back leg with a cushion under that knee, front foot planted, and do exactly the same tuck, squeeze and forward ease from there

Harder

  • Shift more of your weight forward over the front leg, sink a little deeper, or take your hand off the support so you have to balance

2Wake the side
Lateral band walk
10 to 15 steps each way, 2 or 3 roundsA resistance band
Set up
  • The band looped around both legs, just above the knees
  • Feet about hip-width apart
  • A soft bend in the knees and your backside pushed back behind you, so you drop into a low, athletic half-squat
  • A little tension in the band already
The movement

Staying low and level, step one foot out to the side, only a small step, an inch or two, then let the other foot follow, keeping tension in the band the whole time and never quite letting your feet click together. Take several of these small steps one way, then several back the other. The band should be fighting you every inch, and the muscle on the side of your hip should be the thing beating it.

What to get right
  • The work sitting squarely on the side of the hips
  • Feet pointing straight ahead, not turning out
  • The knees not dragged inward as you step
  • Low and level throughout. No bobbing up and down between steps, no swinging your trunk to fling the leg out

Easier

  • The lying hip abduction. On your side, legs straight and stacked, lift the top leg up in line with your body, then lower it slowly. It sits between the clamshell and this one

Harder

  • A heavier band, still kept above the knees
  • Later, once your control is rock solid, the band dropped down to your ankles

3Build the powerhouse
Glute bridge
2 to 3 sets of 10 to 15, controlled, with a real squeeze and a pause at the top of each
Set up
  • Lie on your back, knees bent
  • Feet planted flat on the floor further out from you than feels natural, almost awkwardly far away, rather than tucked in close to your backside
  • Feet about hip-width apart
  • Arms resting by your sides
The movement

Squeeze your glutes and press down through your heels to lift your hips off the floor, until your body makes one straight line from your knees to your shoulders. Squeeze hard at the top, hold for a moment, then lower slowly back down. The lift comes from the glutes driving through the heels, not from cranking your lower back into an arch.

What to get right
  • The lift coming from the back of you, the glutes leading and the hamstrings chipping in behind them
  • Felt in your backside and the back of your thighs, not in the front of your thighs and not as an ache in your lower back
  • Heels planted, toes light
  • A proper held squeeze at the top rather than a quick bounce

Harder

  • One leg at a time, once both together feel easy. A big jump in load, and it evens up your two sides into the bargain
  • Free ankle crossed over the working knee, figure-four style, is the easier of the two
  • Free leg held straight out and aloft is the harder, because the working leg gets no help at all

4Put it on one leg
Single-leg knee drive
8 to 10 slow drives each side, a couple of roundsA chair
Set up
  • Stand tall on one leg, the standing knee soft rather than locked out
  • Bodyweight only to begin with
The movement

Slowly raise the knee of the free leg up towards hip height, hold it there for a beat, then lower it back down under control, without letting that foot touch the floor. That's one. Do your reps, then swap to the other leg.

What to get right
  • The standing hip staying level throughout while the other leg moves
  • The standing ankle still and the foot busy underneath you, gripping and adjusting to hold you steady
  • No swinging the knee up with momentum. Drive it up slowly and lower it slowly
  • The standing knee sitting still and in line, no cave

Easier

  • The still version first. Stand on the one leg with the free foot lifted just clear of the floor, and hold the pelvis dead level, a fingertip on a worktop if you need it. Add the knee drive once that hold feels solid

Harder

  • A light dumbbell held in against your chest
  • Then eyes closed, as you did with the one-leg stand at the ankle
  • Then holding a little longer at the top
