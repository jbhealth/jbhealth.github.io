Part III · Fix the Roots

Knees

From Make a Fuss · Chapter 9
A hinge caught between the ankle below and the hip above, and usually the messenger rather than the cause. Close to how I work with clients: the same fixes, picked for what their own reading turned up and rebuilt as it changes.
⌄
1Settle it
Wall sit
Hold towards 30 seconds, 3 or 4 times overNo kit needed
Set up
  • Back flat against a wall
  • Feet about a stride out in front, hip-width apart
  • Toes pointing forward
  • Arms folded, or resting by your sides
The movement

Slide slowly down the wall, bending your knees, until your thighs are somewhere on the way to parallel with the floor, and hold there. You're not chasing a deep squat, especially not early on. Slide down only as far as you can hold without any sharp complaint from the knee, and stay there for a spell, then slide back up. Rest, and go again.

What to get right
  • Knees tracking in line with the feet, never falling inward
  • Heels planted, weight spread through the whole foot
  • Depth judged by the knee, not by your pride

Easier

  • Don't go as low. A shallow slide held well beats a deep one you can't hold

Harder

  • Sink lower, or hold longer, once the knee has nothing to say about it
⌄
2Wake the steering muscle
Clamshell
2 to 3 sets of 12 to 15 each side, slow and controlledNo kit needed
Set up
  • Lie on your side, on the floor or a bed
  • Knees bent up in front of you to about a right angle, knees and feet stacked one on top of the other
  • Head resting on your lower arm
  • Top hand on the floor in front of you, or on your top hip, to keep yourself steady
The movement

Keeping your feet touching and your hips stacked and still, lift your top knee up and away from the bottom one, opening your legs like a clam, as far as you can go without your hips rolling backward. Pause at the top, then lower the knee slowly back down, and go again. It's a small movement, so make it a slow and deliberate one.

What to get right
  • The work felt on the side and back of your top hip. That's the muscle we're waking up
  • The hips staying stacked and dead still, not rolling back as the knee opens

Harder

  • A band looped around your knees, for more to work against
  • Then the lying hip abduction, which is a different set-up for the same muscle: legs straight and stacked rather than bent, lifting the whole top leg in line with your body
⌄
3Groove the tracking
Sit-to-stand
2 to 3 sets of 8 to 12, controlledA sturdy chair
Set up
  • Sit on the front half of the seat
  • Feet flat on the floor, about hip-width apart and pulled back a little towards you
  • Toes pointing forward or a touch out
The movement

Lean your weight forward over your feet, then stand up, driving through the whole of both feet, without pushing off your hands if you can help it. Stand tall. Then lower yourself slowly back down under control, leading with your backside, sending your hips back to reach for the seat rather than letting your knees drop forward, all the way down, and sit right down onto the seat with your full weight. Not a quick tap of the seat and up again, a proper sit, letting yourself go heavy for a moment. Then stand back up from that dead stop, and go again.

What to get right
  • The knees tracking in line with the feet, roughly above the middle toes, and never falling inward
  • Hips back, not knees forward
  • Slow and deliberate, especially on the way down, with a real pause at the bottom

Easier

  • A higher seat, or a light touch of the hands
  • If a knee is sore or keeps wanting to drift in, hold a soft cushion or a small pillow between your knees and squeeze it gently as you rise and lower

Harder

  • A lower seat, slowing the lower still further, then holding a weight against your chest
⌄
4Load one leg
Split squat
2 to 3 sets of 6 to 10 each leg, controlledSomething within reach for a steadying hand
Set up
  • Stand tall, then step one foot a good stride straight back, into a long split stance
  • Front foot flat, back heel lifted
  • Weight shared fairly evenly between the two feet, roughly fifty-fifty
The movement

Lower yourself straight down, thinking of dropping your back knee towards the floor rather than bending your front knee. Aim that back knee straight down and let the front leg bend to allow it. Go only as far as you can under control and without the knee complaining. Shallow is fine, especially to begin with, and the depth comes as the control does. Then drive back up through both feet to where you started, pushing up through the mid-foot and heel rather than the toes, and keeping the whole foot planted. Do all your reps in that stance, then swap your feet over.

What to get right
  • The front knee tracking in line with the foot as you lower and drive up, never caving inward
  • Screw the front foot out into the floor, and you'll feel the hip take charge of holding the knee in line
  • The whole of the front foot staying in contact with the floor, and the movement straight down and up

Easier

  • Step-ups onto a low, sturdy step. Lead with one leg, driving up through the heel of that foot rather than pushing off the back one, stand tall, then lower yourself back down slowly under control. Keep the step low while the knee is still grumpy, because it's the stepping down that a sore knee tends to object to most

Harder

  • A touch deeper as the knee allows, then a weight held, then evening up your two sides until the weaker one has caught the stronger
