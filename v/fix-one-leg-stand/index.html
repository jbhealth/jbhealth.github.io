Part III · Fix the Roots

Feet and ankles

From Make a Fuss · Chapter 8
Your foundation. When the ankle cannot bend, or will not hold steady, every joint above it covers for it. Close to how I work with clients: the same fixes, picked for what their own reading turned up and rebuilt as it changes.
⌄
1Wake the foot up
Towel scrunch
A minute or so on each foot, once or twice a dayA chair
Set up
  • Sit tall in the chair, shoes and socks off
  • The towel laid flat on the floor in front of you
  • One bare foot on the near end of it, heel on the floor, toes just onto the towel
The movement

Keeping your heel planted, scrunch your toes to grab a bit of the towel and drag it back towards you, then reach the toes out and grab the next bit, pulling the whole towel in under your foot a bunch at a time. Once it's gathered up, push it back out with the toes and go again.

What to get right
  • The heel staying down while the toes do all the work, gripping and reaching
  • The movement staying down in the foot, not the whole leg sliding back to drag the towel in for you
  • The pile of towel gathering as you go. That's your proof, right there in front of you
⌄
2Free the bend
Heel drop off a step
10 to 15 slow drops, once or twice a dayA step
Set up
  • Balls of both feet on the edge of the step, heels hanging off the back
  • A hand on the rail
  • Feet pointing straight ahead, not turned out
The movement

Let both heels sink slowly down below the level of the step, as far as they'll comfortably go, until you feel the stretch build low in the calf and deep in the ankle. Pause a moment at the bottom. Then rise back up onto the balls of your feet, under control, and go again.

What to get right
  • The feet pointing dead straight
  • The weight even across the ball of each foot, not rolling out to the little-toe side
  • Slow down, slow up. It's the slow lower into the bottom that does the freeing, so don't rush it and don't bounce out of it

Easier

  • The ankle dorsiflexion drill from your reading. Heel flat, foot straight, knee driving to the wall and back, until the loaded version feels manageable

Harder

  • One leg at a time. If one ankle came out clearly stiffer, give that side the extra
⌄
3Teach it to hold you
One-leg stand, done properly
Build towards 30 seconds or so on each side, a few times overA wall
Set up
  • Stand tall next to it, feet under your hips
  • Soften the knee that'll take your weight. A slight bend, not locked out
The movement

Shift your weight onto that one leg and lift the other foot just clear of the floor. Hold. Steady yourself with as little help as you can get away with. Then swap legs.

What to get right
  • The foot settled and level across its base, not rolling inward with the arch collapsing, and not rolling out onto its outer edge
  • The work down in the foot and ankle, the small muscles gripping and easing to keep you level

Easier

  • A fingertip on the worktop, or a whole hand if you need it. Take the help until you don't need it

Harder

  • Eyes closed. A bigger step than it sounds, because you lose the steadying your eyes were doing, so keep a hand near your support
  • Eyes open, reaching the free foot out in front, then to the side, then behind, like the hand of a clock, while the standing foot holds you level
  • Standing on something soft, a cushion or a folded towel, so the foot has to work harder to find its footing
⌄
4Give the shin its due
Tibialis raise
2 to 3 sets of about 15, slow and controlledA wall to lean back against
Set up
  • Back against the wall, or a few inches out and leaning lightly back on it
  • Heels a little way out from the skirting, so you feel steady
  • Feet flat, weight back through your heels
The movement

Keeping your heels planted, lift your toes and the fronts of your feet up towards your shins, as high as they'll go, then lower them back down slowly, under control.

What to get right
  • The lift coming from the front of the foot, toes leading, heels staying put
  • Down slowly. Don't let the feet flop back to the floor
  • Both feet working evenly

Easier

  • Sitting in a chair, feet flat, lifting the toes and forefeet while the heels stay down

Harder

  • Walking the length of the hallway and back up on your heels, toes held high. The same job with your bodyweight behind it
