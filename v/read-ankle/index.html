Knee to wall: reading your ankle

How far your knee can travel forward over your foot before the heel lifts. It takes thirty seconds, and it tells you a lot, because a stiff ankle rarely stays an ankle problem for long.

1 min 7 sec · watch on YouTube if it won't play here

You'll need a clear bit of wall. That's all.

How to do it

  • Stand with one foot forward, toes right against the wall and pointing straight at it. Other foot a step behind, for balance. Hands on the wall to steady you.
  • Keeping that front heel flat on the floor, bend the knee forward to touch the wall. At this range it's easy.
  • Now shuffle the front foot back a little and go again, heel still down. Then back a bit more.
  • Keep edging back until the knee will only just touch with the heel staying flat. That gap between your toes and the wall is your limit.
  • Note where it is, then do the other side.

What to watch

  • The heel. The instant it peels off to let the knee reach, you've found your edge.
  • The foot. Pointing straight at the wall, not turning its toes out to sneak a bit more.

What good looks like

About a hand's width, near enough 10cm, is a fair target for a healthy ankle. Do both sides. They're often different, and the difference matters as much as the number.

You're reading the ankle here, not forcing it. Keep it gentle the whole way.

Why a stiff ankle matters further up

When the ankle can't bend, the joints above it cover for it, and that's how a stiff ankle turns into a sore knee or a tired hip. The film shows you the test. The book explains what it's telling you and what to do about it.
