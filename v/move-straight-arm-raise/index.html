The push

From Make a Fuss · Chapter 14
The push is sending something away from you, or pushing yourself up off the ground. Close to how I work with clients: the same six patterns, loaded to suit them and moved on as they get stronger.
⌄
1Press-up
2 to 3 sets of 8 to 12No kit needed
Set up
  • Hands a little wider than your shoulders, on whichever surface you're using
  • Body in one straight line from head to heels, middle braced
  • The further your feet are from the surface, the harder it is
The movement

Lower your chest towards the surface under control, until it's a few inches away or gently touching. Then press yourself back up until your arms are straight. Slow down, honest up.

What to get right
  • One straight line, head to heels. No sagging in the middle and no backside in the air
  • Elbows travelling back at about forty-five degrees, not flared straight out to the sides
  • The whole range. A short, bouncy press-up builds a short, bouncy press-up

Easier

  • Move up the ladder, not down: a wall first, then the edge of a worktop, then a sturdy bench, then the floor. Each step is the same exercise at a kinder angle

Harder

  • Lower the surface, and eventually the floor
  • Then a floor press or a bench press with weights, though you'll not need that for a long while
⌄
2Overhead press
2 to 3 sets of 8 to 12A pair of dumbbells
Set up
  • Stand tall, feet about hip-width
  • Weights at shoulder height, palms facing forward or slightly in
  • Middle braced, ribs down, so your lower back can't arch to help
The movement

Press the weights straight up overhead until your arms are at full stretch, then lower them slowly back to your shoulders. Press up, not forward.

What to get right
  • Shoulder blades set, shoulders down and long. No shrugging up towards your ears
  • Ribs down throughout. If your back arches to get the weight up, the weight is too heavy
  • Slow on the way down. That's where the strength is built

Easier

  • One arm at a time, so you can watch it
  • Or go back and live on the straight-arm raise from the shoulder chapter a while longer. The control comes first, and the strength is built on top of it

Harder

  • More weight, grown by the rules
  • A barbell in a gym, once the dumbbells are easy and honest
⌄
3Straight-arm raise
2 to 3 sets of 8 to 12, slow and cleanA light plate
Set up
  • Stand tall, feet about hip-width
  • Middle braced gently, the way you learned at the back, so your ribs don't flare and your lower back doesn't arch to help
  • Arms hanging down in front of you, elbows soft
  • Shoulder blades set down and back
The movement

Keep a soft, slight bend in your elbows the whole way, never locked out straight, the same easy unlock you keep in your knees. Raise the weight slowly up in front of you, on up past shoulder height and all the way overhead until you are reaching tall. Then lower it just as slowly back down under full control. As it comes overhead, let it settle a touch in front of straight up rather than forcing it back behind your head. Slow up, slow down, and no swinging it.

What to get right
  • The shoulder blades gliding round and up as your arms rise, and no shrug
  • The shoulders staying down and long the whole way
  • The weight a touch in front of dead overhead, where the shoulder has the most room and the least pinch
  • The lift coming from your shoulders, not from leaning back and swinging the weight up

Easier

  • Raise only to the height where your shoulders stay down and easy, even if that's no higher than shoulder height to begin with, and let the top come back as the control grows
  • Or go back and live on the wall slide a while longer, then return to this

Harder

  • A light weight in each hand, once the shared-weight version feels easy, so each shoulder works on its own and the weaker side can have the extra it needs
  • Then, once that overhead reach is strong and clean, letting the weight travel a little past vertical at the top, easing it gently back beyond straight overhead so the shoulder gets a soft stretch at the very end of its range while it's still under load
